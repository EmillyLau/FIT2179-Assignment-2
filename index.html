Flood risk and climate vulnerability in Malaysia

Geographic overview

Where Floods Strike Hardest

Start with place. The first pattern is spatial: Kelantan and Terengganu repeatedly show heavier household burden.

Why it matters: loss per household reveals vulnerability that raw state totals can hide.

Displacement over time

Flood disruption repeats across years

Each bubble shows a state-year flood record. Larger bubbles reveal years where more people were directly affected.

Why it matters: the timeline shows whether flood burden is isolated or recurring across states.

Climate signal

A Wetter, Hotter Malaysia

Flood risk is not one storm. Rainfall varies sharply, while maximum temperature rises more steadily.

Why it matters: rising heat adds background stress even when rainfall changes year by year.

Seasonality

The monsoon belt floods in season

Darker months cluster around November to January for east coast states. Sabah and Sarawak show broader exposure.

Why it matters: flood preparedness needs to follow both seasonal and year-round patterns.

Rainfall anomaly

Above-average rainfall years became flood years

Rainfall anomaly separates ordinary wet years from unusually wet flood seasons.

Why it matters: major flood years align with rainfall above the long-term average.

Human and economic impact

Lives, Livelihoods, and Losses

Kelantan and Terengganu lead total losses. The donut explains what was damaged.

Why it matters: users compare total loss first, then inspect household, business, and infrastructure damage.

Rainfall alone does not predict loss

The scatterplot compares rainfall with damage, then separates states by region.

Flood duration changes response needs

Short floods can be sudden. Longer floods strain evacuation and recovery.

Composite vulnerability

Which States Are Most at Risk?

The final chart brings the evidence together. It summarises exposure, loss, and human disruption.

Why it matters: vulnerability is highest where climate exposure and social disruption overlap.

Key takeaways

What the Dashboard Shows

The evidence points to a simple conclusion: flood risk is both environmental and social.

Geography

East coast states show the clearest household burden.

Climate

Flood timing follows a strong monsoon pattern.

Losses

Economic damage depends on exposure, not rainfall alone.

Planning

Resilience should target households and displacement together.

About This Visualisation

This dashboard examines Malaysian flood vulnerability through geography, climate patterns, economic loss, and human impact. It was created for FIT2179 Data Visualisation at Monash University Malaysia.
